## What this policy is for

DMARC Harbor sits on the path between a domain owner and the mail servers that
deliver its email. The Service enforces nothing itself: it reads what receiving
servers publish and shows you. But a tool that helps you block mail could be used
to harass someone, so we set out what we will not help you do.

This policy forms part of our Terms of Service.

## Prohibited uses

You may not use the Service, or allow anyone to use it on your behalf, to:

1. **Send, relay, forge or spoof email.** The Service must not be used to send
   mail as anyone, to relay mail through infrastructure you do not own, or to
   construct a message whose origin is misleading.
2. **Block someone else's legitimate mail deliberately.** Using DMARC to suppress
   a competitor's or a third party's legitimate mail is not a permitted use of the
   Service.
3. **Harass, intimidate or retaliate against a person or organisation.** Including
   using spoofing findings to target an individual.
4. **Conduct phishing, credential harvesting or social engineering** of any kind.
5. **Stolen credentials or session tokens** from any third party, including from a
   client you manage.
6. **Infringe intellectual property, trade marks or contractual rights** of a third
   party, including in the way you deploy or brand the Service.
7. **Upload or collect personal data** for a domain you are not authorised to
   monitor, or monitor a domain without the owner's permission.
8. **Circumvent technical restrictions**, including rate limits, entitlement
   checks, or the boundaries between workspaces.
9. **Probe or test the vulnerability** of the Service or its infrastructure without
   our prior written permission.
10. **Reverse engineer, decompile or create derivative works** of the Service,
    except where that restriction cannot lawfully be applied.
11. **Resell or sublicense the Service** as a bureau service, or provide it to
    third parties, without a written agreement with us.
12. **Misrepresent the output.** Presenting the Service's findings as a third-party
    audit, certification or attestation is prohibited.

## What is permitted

- Monitoring the domains of clients who have engaged you and who understand what
  you are doing on their behalf.
- Publishing DMARC policy at `p=none` first and increasing enforcement gradually,
  which is the approach the Service recommends.
- Using aggregate report data to investigate sending sources.
- Using forensic reports you are authorised to access, within the retention and
  identity settings you have chosen.
- Publishing a share link or a Trust Center page so a client can read their own
  result.

## Shared responsibility for agency use

If you manage domains for clients, you are responsible for your clients' use of
the Service. When you add a client, you confirm you are authorised to do so on
their behalf.

You control the boundaries of your workspace. DMARC Harbor enforces tenant
isolation: a workspace cannot read another's domains, reports or shares. Keep it
that way.

## Security research

We welcome security research that:

- is conducted on infrastructure you own or have explicit written permission to
  test,
- avoids privacy violations and does not access other customers' data,
- does not degrade the Service for others, and
- gives us reasonable time to fix an issue before public disclosure.

Contact `[SECURITY EMAIL]`. Do not include customer data in a report.

## Enforcement

Where we find a breach we will, depending on severity:

- warn you and ask you to fix it,
- suspend a specific domain or workspace,
- suspend access pending investigation, or
- terminate the subscription, with a refund of the unused period where the breach
  was severe and repeated.

Where we suspend or terminate for a breach that is not a security research report,
we will tell you which clause of this policy was breached.

We will warn and give an opportunity to fix a first breach that is not severe. We
will not warn where continued access would harm another person or organisation.
